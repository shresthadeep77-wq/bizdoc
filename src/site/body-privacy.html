This page describes exactly what the app does with what you type. It is short because the app does very little.

By default, nothing leaves your device

PI & PO Maker is a static page. Unless you sign in, there is no backend it talks to, no API it posts to and nowhere for your documents to be sent. Businesses, customers, products and documents are written to localStorage in your own browser, under a single key, on the device you are using.

No account needed, no tracking

  • You never have to sign up or log in. Signing in is optional, and only needed to keep a copy in sync across devices (see below).
  • No third-party analytics or telemetry service is loaded. Nothing is sent to Google Analytics, Plausible, Sentry or anything like them.
  • The app sets no cookies and no tracking identifiers. If you sign in, your sign-in session is kept in this browser's storage so you stay signed in; it is not used for anything else.
  • There are no advertising or social scripts on the page.

The app does keep a usage log — on your device

So that there is some way to see what is being used and what is breaking, the app writes a short record of its own activity into your browser, alongside your documents. It never leaves the device, because there is no server for it to go to.

Each entry is an action name, a time, and a couple of small details — for example “document created, type PI, 4 lines”, or “a search happened”. Uncaught errors are recorded the same way, so a fault can be seen rather than guessed at.

What is never written to it: document contents, line items or amounts; customer names, addresses or tax numbers; product names or rates; your business details; and the text of anything you type, including search boxes — only that a search took place.

You can read the whole log, export it, change how long it is kept, or switch the recording off entirely and delete what is stored. Your documents are not affected either way.

The things that can leave your device

Each one is off until you start it.

Signing in to sync (optional)

If you choose Continue with Google, Google confirms who you are and the app receives your name, email address and profile picture — nothing else from your Google account. From then on your businesses, customers, products and documents are also stored in a private database (hosted by Supabase) so they appear on every device you sign in on. Every row is tied to your account and the database itself refuses to show it to anyone else; this is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the app.

While signed in, a copy stays in this browser so the app keeps working offline. Changes made offline upload automatically once the connection is back. The usage log described above is never uploaded. Signing out shows this device's own data again; you can also choose to remove the account's copy from the device when you sign out.

One thing to know: the app is hosted on github.io, and pages published under the same GitHub user name share one browser storage area. That includes the sign-in session. Only sign in on a device you trust, and sign out on shared computers.

Google Drive backup

If — and only if — you press Back up to Google Drive, the app loads Google's sign-in script, asks for your permission, and writes one JSON file into your own Drive. It requests the drive.file scope, which is the narrowest one available: it can see and change only the file it created itself, and has no access to anything else in your Drive. Restoring reads that same file back. Google's own handling of it is covered by their privacy policy, not this one.

WhatsApp sharing

The WhatsApp share button opens WhatsApp with a message prefilled. Nothing is sent until you choose a recipient and press send yourself.

The other side of that bargain

If you have not signed in, nothing is on a server, so nobody can recover your data for you. If you clear your browsing data, uninstall the browser, or lose the device, the records go with it. Storage is also per browser and per device — Chrome on your laptop and Safari on your phone are two separate sets of data.

So take backups. Export a backup file somewhere safe on a schedule that matches how much work you would hate to retype.

Deleting everything

Clearing site data for this page in your browser's settings removes every trace of it from that browser, the usage log included. If you never signed in, there is no copy anywhere else. If you did, deleting a record in the app removes it from every device and marks it deleted in your account; its last version is kept there, out of sight, so a mistaken delete can be recovered. To have your account and everything in it erased for good, ask the maintainer through the project's GitHub page.
