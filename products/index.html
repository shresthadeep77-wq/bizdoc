Categories to any depth

Categories are a real tree, not a single flat dropdown. A product can sit at Fittings › Brass › 15 mm, and the picker walks down branch by branch. You can stop at whatever level you mean and confirm there, which is what makes a customer's discount rule able to cover a whole branch or one narrow shelf of it.

Changing many prices at once

Select any number of products — with a filter, a search, or the select-all box — and update them together. Update rates applies a percentage rise or fall, or sets a flat rate, across the whole selection at once. It is the difference between a supplier's 4% increase taking a minute and taking an afternoon.

The same multi-select drives bulk category moves and bulk deletes, and the count of what you have selected stays visible so you always know the blast radius before you confirm.

Two ways to look at the catalogue

  • List — dense rows for scanning part numbers and rates.
  • Grouped by category — the tree, collapsed, for finding something when you only half-remember what it was called.

Long catalogues render in chunks as you scroll, so several thousand products stay responsive on a phone rather than freezing the page while everything draws.

CSV import and export

The whole catalogue moves in and out as CSV. Import shows a preview of the parsed rows and the column mapping before writing anything, so a mismatched header is caught at that point instead of after it has merged into your live data. A blank template with the exact expected columns is one click away, and the parser handles quoted fields with embedded commas and line breaks properly.
